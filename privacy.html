Privacy policy

Last updated: 6 May 2026

GridKit is an iOS app that displays personal KPI data — steps, workouts, activity minutes, manual counts — as a contribution-graph heatmap. This page explains what data we receive, where it goes, how long it stays, and what choices you have.

Short version: Apple Health data never leaves your iPhone. Strava data is fetched directly from Strava using your own read-only OAuth token (stored in your iOS Keychain) and cached on-device only. Our small Cloudflare Worker exists only to relay the OAuth handshake — it doesn't store, analyze, or transmit your data to anyone.

Who we are

GridKit is built and operated by Tony Gao ("we", "us", "our") as a sole-proprietor software project. You can reach us at t.gao@yale.edu. This policy covers the GridKit iOS app and our auxiliary Cloudflare Worker that supports it.

Data we receive, by source

Apple Health (HealthKit)

With your explicit permission, GridKit reads daily aggregates from HealthKit: step count and workout duration. These reads happen entirely inside the iOS HealthKit framework on your device.

  • Data leaves your device: Never. We do not transmit HealthKit data anywhere.
  • Storage: Daily aggregates are cached as a JSON file inside GridKit's app-group container, which is shared only between the GridKit app and its home-screen / lock-screen widget extension on your device — no other app can read it. The cache is removed when you uninstall GridKit.
  • Permission: You grant access via the standard iOS HealthKit prompt. You can revoke it at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → GridKit.

Strava

With your explicit permission, GridKit uses Strava's OAuth flow to receive a read-only access token scoped to activity:read. We use that token to fetch your activity list and compute daily activity-minute totals.

  • What we read: Activity ID, type, start time, and elapsed time — only for the purpose of rendering a daily activity-minutes heatmap. We do not read GPS routes, heart-rate streams, segment efforts, or social graph data.
  • Token storage: The access token is stored in your iOS Keychain (encrypted at rest, protected by your device passcode/biometrics). It never leaves your device after the OAuth handshake.
  • Disconnect: Use Settings → Integrations → Disconnect Strava in the app, or revoke GridKit at strava.com/settings/apps.

Manual entries

When you tap to log a manual count, the entry is written to GridKit's local SwiftData (SQLite) store inside the app's private container. It is never transmitted off-device.

Our backend (Cloudflare Worker)

GridKit operates a small server-side function on Cloudflare Workers whose only job is to complete the Strava OAuth token exchange. This is necessary because Strava requires a client_secret during the OAuth handshake, and that secret cannot safely ship inside an iOS app binary.

The Worker:

  • Receives the OAuth code from Strava.
  • Exchanges it with Strava for an access token, using the client secret it holds.
  • Hands the token back to your device via a deep-link redirect.
  • Does not log access tokens, request bodies, or response bodies.
  • Does not retain any state about you between requests. There is no database; the Worker is stateless.
  • Does not track, profile, or analytics-instrument users.

Cloudflare itself, as our infrastructure provider, may log basic request metadata (IP address, request timestamp) for abuse-prevention and operational purposes per Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Data we do not collect

  • We do not run analytics or telemetry SDKs in the app.
  • We do not collect device identifiers (IDFA, IDFV) for tracking purposes.
  • We do not collect crash reports automatically. (If you opt-in to share diagnostics with Apple via iOS Settings, those are handled by Apple, not us.)
  • We do not have user accounts. No email, no password, no profile.
  • We do not sell, rent, or share data with third parties for any purpose.
  • We do not show ads, and there is no ad network embedded.

Sharing and disclosure

We do not share your personal data with third parties, except:

  • Service providers necessary to operate the app: Cloudflare (the Worker host) and Strava (your own Strava account, via OAuth). In both cases, the data flow is your device ↔ that service; we are not in the middle.
  • If required by law: If we receive a valid legal demand we cannot in good conscience refuse, we may disclose what we have. Note that what we have is essentially nothing — there are no user records on our side to produce.

Retention

All on-device caches live for as long as the app is installed and you remain connected to a given integration. Disconnecting an integration clears its cached data. Uninstalling GridKit removes everything.

Our Worker retains nothing between requests.

Children

GridKit is not directed to children under 13 (or under 16 in the EU/UK). If you are under that age, do not use GridKit. We do not knowingly receive data from children.

Your rights

Because we operate no user database and store no personal data on our servers, the practical mechanism for exercising your data rights is on your device:

  • Access & portability: Your data lives in the source services (Apple Health, Strava) — those services provide their own export tools.
  • Deletion: Disconnect each integration in Settings → Integrations, then uninstall GridKit. All on-device caches are removed.
  • Revocation: Revoke our OAuth grant directly at strava.com/settings/apps.

For any other request, write to t.gao@yale.edu.

Security

OAuth tokens are stored in the iOS Keychain (encrypted at rest by the Secure Enclave). All network traffic uses HTTPS. The Cloudflare Worker uses signed CSRF state tokens during the OAuth handshake to prevent authorization-code injection attacks.

No system is perfect. If you discover a vulnerability, please report it privately to t.gao@yale.edu — we'll credit you in the fix.

International users

The Cloudflare Worker runs on Cloudflare's global edge network. Your OAuth handshake transits Cloudflare's nearest data center to you. Because we don't store data, there is no cross-border transfer of personal data attributable to GridKit beyond the round-trip required to complete OAuth.

Changes to this policy

If we make material changes (we add a new integration, change what we read, etc.), we'll update this page and update the "Last updated" date at the top. For substantive changes affecting how data is used, we'll surface a one-time in-app notice the next time you open GridKit.
